## Topic

When someone is injured or killed in a road accident, the family can claim compensation from a Motor Accident Claims Tribunal under section 166 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988. These claims are usually made against the vehicle owner and the insurer, so the insurer's liability (including defences under s 149, such as a driver's licence problem) is often the real fight. I chose this topic because these cases affect ordinary people directly, and the Supreme Court's rulings decide how quickly and how fully victims are paid.

## How we found the judgments

I looked for reasoned Supreme Court of India judgments on s 166 compensation and insurer liability decided between 1 September 2021 and 31 August 2026. I used Claude to suggest candidate cases and their citations, then opened every case on Indian Kanoon and CaseMine to confirm the case name, date, bench, outcome and holding. Search terms used:"section 166 Motor Vehicles Act". Results reviewed: 21. I included only reasoned judgments, not orders without reasons, and ended with 7 judgments. The source links in the dataset point to Indian Kanoon or CaseMine, not to the Court's own copy.

## What the data shows

The dataset is in `data.xlsx` and `data.csv`. The fuller analysis is on the project page linked above.

## Judgments per year.

Two judgments are from 2022, one from 2024, three from 2025 and one from 2026. There are none from 2021 or 2023. What surprised me is how much of the set falls in 2025, and that two of the 2025 to 2026 entries are suo motu matters in which the Court gave general directions.

## Outcome.

Four of the seven judgments allowed an appeal, one dismissed an appeal and two are coded "Other", because they are suo motu matters that end in directions and not in allowing or dismissing anything. The dismissed appeal was brought by an insurer, which fits the pattern of the Court not accepting technical defences.

## Who won.

Individual claimants or licence holders won four times. Three cases are "Mixed or unclear", and no company or public body is recorded as an outright winner. This did not surprise me, because the cases lean towards protecting victims, but it may partly reflect which cases I picked.

## Limits

Seven judgments are a very small and hand-picked sample, so nothing here shows how the Supreme Court decides motor accident cases in general. The year counts can change a lot if a few more judgments are added, and some coding choices are my own judgment calls. One case in the set, the Constitution Bench decision on LMV licences, deals with licensing and feeds into the s 149 insurer defence, so it is related to s 166 claims but is not a direct s 166 case.
